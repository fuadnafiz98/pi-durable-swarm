00 · CATALOGHow to read a grammar catalog

Every figure on this page draws one real fact of the pi-durable session in a different diagram grammar. The receipts are inherited from the session page (docs/pidurable), where each was read in the vendored sources; here the subject never changes so that the language is all you compare. The caption under each figure states when to reach for that grammar — and its honest limits.

The test a grammar must pass: it shows what prose cannot — a boundary, what moves, or what changes. A grammar that only lists names fails; the same boxes-and-arrows redrawn with a different choreography is still just one grammar. Ten families follow: node-link, flow and sequence, state, structure, data, time, runtime, comparison, and domain metaphors. The last section draws one round twelve ways — the same submit → settle fact in twelve languages, back to back.

01 · NODE-LINKWhat talks to what, drawn eleven ways

The reference grammar — boxes inside regions, arrows saying what travels. Everything after it is an alternative to this picture.

g01 · regions and boundaries. The reference: organs inside one harness, the outside world hatched. Reach for it when containment and one durability boundary are the story.

g02 · topology small-multiples. Four mini panels: star, ring, bus, mesh. Reach for it when the shape of connections is the fact (here: how processes can share one storage contract).

g03 · bipartite columns. Two kinds of thing, one kind of edge. Here the ownership edge: every tool task owns exactly one child conversation (src/types.ts:306-315).

g04 · adjacency matrix. Who resumes whom as a filled cell grid — better than arrows when the relations are many and typed (src/harness/scheduler.ts:1096-1112).

g05 · arc diagram. Nodes on one axis, relations as arcs. Reach for it when everything hangs off one spine — here, submissions against the transcript (src/types.ts:383-414).

g06 · radial tree. One root, children fanning out on a circle. Same lab-02 graph as the org-chart below; choose radial when depth is the message (src/harness/generation.ts:697-710).

g07 · circuit diagram. Signals, gates, junction dots. The pump's enable line as a circuit: four switches wired to one gate (src/harness/scheduler.ts:318-321).

g08 · orbits. Everything trusted inside the disk; foreign agents ride outside the rim, connected by one seam. Motion shows the boundary holding (src/env/index.ts).

g09 · hypercube of booleans. Three independent flags as cube axes; every corner is a real state of one run (src/types.ts:505-536).

g10 · nested regions. Containment drawn literally: each scope strictly inside the one above (src/documents.ts:39-51).

g11 · metro map. Lines are conversations, stations are rounds, interchanges are tool branches. Reach for it when routes matter more than timing (src/harness/agent.ts:69).

g12 · chord ends. A spine with chords to four adapters: one contract, four shapes, one process at a time (src/storage/sqlite/cloudflare.ts:187-197).

02 · FLOW & SEQUENCEIn what order, told eleven ways

Order is the easiest thing to draw and the easiest thing to draw badly: stations that never say what travels, swimlanes that never say who acts. Eleven grammars for order.

g13 · stations in a row. The reference: seven stations, done stations check off, the current one lights. The bread-and-butter step figure (src/harness/submissions.ts:224-239).

g14 · swimlanes. One lane per actor, time left to right: the same round split by who runs each step. Use it when hand-offs, not steps, are the story (src/harness/submissions.ts:224-239).

g15 · sequence lifelines. Vertical lifelines, messages top-down, returns marked. Use it when the exact call-and-return discipline matters (src/types.ts:843).

g16 · bpmn events and gateways. Start and end events, one gateway deciding the fork. Use it for processes with conditional branches a human signs off (src/harness/agent.ts:69).

g17 · conveyor. The round as work on a moving belt; rollers are stations. Motion is literal — a token rides the belt (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g18 · fork and join. One parent splits to three parallel children and joins back — the tool round without a sequence pretence (src/types.ts:519-525).

g19 · pipeline with buffers. Stages separated by bounded queues. Use it when rates differ between stages — here, the model stream and the 100 ms commit throttle (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g20 · waterfall steps. Each step a stair, each stair dropping one receipt into committed storage. Use it when every step's point is what it left behind (src/harness/generation.ts:181-199).

g21 · comic strip. Four frames, one sentence each, drawn like panels. Reach for it when the story is a drama — a kill and a comeback — not a mechanism.

g22 · ladder diagram. Relay-logic rungs: contacts in series and parallel feeding one coil. The pump's enable as a rung (src/harness/scheduler.ts:1096-1112).

g23 · interaction overview. A flowchart whose inline block is a sequence fragment. Use it to bridge two granularities without two full diagrams.

03 · STATE & BEHAVIORWhat states, what moves between them?

Ten grammars for the task life — five states (src/types.ts:505-536) and five outcomes (src/types.ts:465-499). The state machine is the reference; the rest answer questions a state machine cannot.

g24 · state machine. The reference: states as boxes, transitions labelled with their trigger. Pick a state to light its outgoing edges (src/harness/scheduler.ts:396-414).

g25 · statechart with nesting. A state that is itself a machine: running contains streaming and tool round. Use it when one state has internal gears (src/types.ts:512-525).

g26 · transition matrix. From-states down, to-states across, allowed moves filled. The complete map of legal moves in one glance.

g27 · petri net. Places are states, tokens are invocations, a transition consumes then produces (src/types.ts:512-517).

g28 · decision table. Conditions across the top, outcomes down, one rule per column — the restart scan as a truth table (src/harness/scheduler.ts:296-312).

g29 · karnaugh-style truth grid. Two booleans on a 2×2: tool replay safety versus what a crash already owns (src/harness/tool.ts).

g30 · decision tree. Classify's fork as a tree: tool calls or text, then per tool: replay, ownership, scope (src/harness/generation.ts (classify)).

g31 · outcome quadrant. The five terminal outcomes on two axes: expected versus broken, finished versus stopped (src/types.ts:465-499).

g32 · phase diagram. Regions over a crash point: what is already committed, what dies with the invocation, what gets retried (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g33 · classic flowchart. Diamonds and connectors — the original grammar, kept for one message: the loop at the bottom is the only legal way back (src/types.ts:148-150).

04 · STRUCTUREWhat is built on what, what contains what?

Structure grammars answer "what holds what up". The isometric stack is the reference; twelve languages after it, from rock strata to star schemas.

g34 · isometric stack. The reference: layers resting on layers, depth meaning weight (src/harness/registry.ts).

g35 · geological strata. Storage as rock: the journal and nine tables in strata that only ever settle downward (src/storage/sqlite/storage.ts:191-201).

g36 · directory tree. The harness's modules as a file tree. Use it when the reader will open the source next (src/harness).

g37 · outline tree. The nine tables grouped by role, indented. Text-first structure — the forge of catalog pages (src/storage/sqlite/schema.ts:5-16).

g38 · treemap. Area encodes writes per commit. The submission round's nine tables as one rectangle, subdivided (src/storage/sqlite/schema.ts:5-16).

g39 · sunburst rings. The same hierarchy as g37, radial: session at the centre, groups as inner arcs, tables as outer arcs.

g40 · org chart. The harness's organs reporting to one session line — the org chart's honest use: reporting, not hierarchy theatre.

g41 · exploded view. One commit pulled apart into its five ledger stages, floating above the file (src/storage/sqlite/storage.ts:191-201).

g42 · blueprint with dimensions. The submission record drawn to scale with dimension arrows for its fields (src/types.ts:371-376).

g43 · anatomy. The thing itself, drawn as its fields, each annotated with its receipt (src/types.ts:383-414).

g44 · entity relationships. Cards and crow's-feet: one conversation, many entries; one task, one child conversation (src/types.ts:306-315).

g45 · star schema. entries as the fact table; conversations, tasks and documents as dimensions around it (src/storage/sqlite/schema.ts:5-16).

05 · DATA STRUCTURESWhat shape does the durable state take?

Data-structure grammars draw the memory discipline itself — indexes, logs, rings, lattices — so the reader sees the cost inside the correctness.

g46 · b-tree. The record-ids index as a two-level tree: one root page, leaves holding ranges (src/storage/sqlite/schema.ts:5-16).

g47 · lsm memtable flush. Streamed partials landing as an in-memory table flushed to durable runs — lsm thinking applied to the 100 ms throttle (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g48 · hash buckets. requestId dedupe as buckets: the key hashes to exactly one bucket, so a retry finds its original (src/types.ts:374-375).

g49 · ring buffer. watchEvents as a ring over a conversation: new events overwrite the oldest read (src/harness/events.ts:69-120).

g50 · write-ahead strip. The session line as a metered tape: every commit advances the seq, everything before it is settled (src/storage/sqlite/storage.ts:191-201).

g51 · vector clock. Three conversations each keeping their own entry count — concurrency without a global clock (src/harness/live.ts:111).

g52 · merkle ladder. Document revisions hash-chaining upward: each revision commits to the one before (src/documents.ts:39-41).

g53 · crdt lattice. Concurrent writes as nodes in a partial order; the join is monotone. A session doc's writes under concurrent workers.

g54 · quorum circles. Read set and write set as overlapping circles; the overlap is correctness. Drawn for a future fleet of cells (README.md:669).

g55 · queue with heads. The inbox as a tape with head and cut-off markers: waiting, admitted, placed (src/harness/inbox.ts:99-111).

06 · TIMEWhat happens when, and what survives?

Time grammars put a ruler under the story. The lane clock is the reference; nine languages after, from gantt bars to a two-processor strip chart.

g56 · lane clock. The reference: three lanes, one axis, events that pause the clock. Press play for the healthy round; switch scenarios for the SIGKILL.

the receipts behind the clock

Lane facts inherit from the session page: the SIGKILL landed with 144 streamed chars unflushed; the reopened process demoted the stale run and re-ran it — 2 550 chars committed on the second attempt (lab 05 · src/harness/scheduler.ts:296-312).

g57 · gantt. Lab-05's retried attempt as bars with dependencies: reopen ends where demotion starts, resume ends where retry starts.

g58 · departure board. Attempts as a split-flap board: ATTEMPT 1 · KILLED · 144 CHARS LOST; ATTEMPT 2 · COMMITTED · 2 550 CHARS.

g59 · seismograph. Partial commits every ~100 ms as regular spikes; the kill as one outsized jolt that ends the trace (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g60 · burn-down of the uncommitted tail. Streamed characters as a growing line, committed as a staircase lagging 100 ms behind; the gap at the kill is the loss.

g61 · interval bars. One bar per 100 ms window, provider bytes shaded, the committed slice solid. Length encodes the partialIntervalMs contract (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g62 · lineage ledger. Entries stamped by id in a vertical ledger, with the submission's entries bracketed — transcript as disturbed-by-nothing order.

g63 · retry ladder. Attempts as rungs up a wall; the fall marks the kill, the landing the resume. Same story as the clock, re-told for one glance.

g64 · strip chart handover. Two processes share one axis; the cutover is a single vertical rule with both process names — who ran what, in order.

07 · RUNTIMEWho runs what, frame by frame?

Runtime grammars show execution: stacks, flames, traces, mailboxes. Their shared lesson on this page — frames vanish, state stays.

g65 · call stack. The reference: frames bottom-up, the top running, the note beside it. Step the frames of one submit (src/harness/types.ts:90-104).

g66 · flame bars. One row of nested bars: drain contains the task's phases; width is time share. The 100 ms commit is one thin entry (src/harness/agent.ts:35).

g67 · waterfall trace. Dev-tools spans with start and end offsets on one axis — the same drain with absolute times (src/harness/scheduler.ts:1102-1112).

g68 · actor mailbox. The submission queue as an actor's mailbox; waits and answers come back stamped (src/harness/inbox.ts:99-111).

g69 · event loop ring. The drain as the loop's recurring tick; waits and aborts enter via the incoming edge (src/harness/scheduler.ts:1102-1112).

g70 · csp channel. Writers on one end of a channel, the ledger on the other; a commit is one message, accepted whole.

g71 · worker pool. Three tool tasks in a pool, one slot parked waiting: concurrency with visible capacity (src/types.ts:519-525).

g72 · heap generations. Committed state as the old generation that survives every collection; the unflushed stream tail as the nursery that dies (lab 05).

08 · COMPARISONWhich option, what changed?

Comparison grammars put two truths in one viewport and mark the delta. Seven ways, ending with the catalog comparing itself.

g73 · before and after overlay. One canvas, two inks: the memory-only round ghosted dashed, the committed round solid. Pick a side.

g74 · side by side. Two canvases, one fact each: foreground tool children versus background reporters (lab 02 · lab 03).

g75 · diff marks. The healthy and killed rounds overlaid with diff marks: checks where they agree, crosses where the crash costs.

g76 · the catalog quadrant. Six grammars placed on fidelity versus authoring cost — the catalog judging itself, with the taxonomy as the plot.

g77 · adapter scatter. Four storage adapters on latency versus durability guarantees; the handbook's first chart for any "which one?" question (README.md:669).

g78 · phase transition. The pump's paused and running states as regions along an axis of call-ins, with the transition edge steep and labelled.

g79 · traffic lights. A grid of green, grey and red dots: which call-ins resume the pump, which drain it, which do neither (src/harness/harness.ts:244-290).

09 · DOMAIN METAPHORSWhat if the machine had a body?

Metaphor grammars borrow a physical system the reader already understands — but only when the borrowed invariant is real. Each below states the borrowed invariant it actually uses.

g80 · patch panel. The hub as a switchboard patch bay: each conversation one port, callers patched in and out. Borrowed invariant: connections are explicit and auditable (src/harness/types.ts:90-104).

g81 · plumbing. The session line as one pipe with valves; commits flow one way. Borrowed invariant: pressure and one-way flow (src/session/session.ts).

g82 · orchestra. One conductor (the router), sections (workers), one shared score (the session doc). Borrowed invariant: parts differentiate, the score synchronizes (src/documents.ts:39-51).

g83 · assembly line. A checkpoint car moves down the line; each station stamps one more field. Borrowed invariant: the chassis accumulates (src/harness/generation.ts:181-199).

g84 · warehouse aisles. Nine tables as aisles and shelves; the commit is the forklift that never leaves a box half-emptied. Borrowed invariant: units of move (src/storage/sqlite/schema.ts:5-16).

g85 · postal sorting. Inputs as letters routed into bins by one sorter; requestId is the already-stamped letter that re-files itself. Borrowed invariant: sorting by key (src/types.ts:374-375).

g86 · elevator shafts. Task states as floors, the invocation as the cabin. A restart rebuilds the shaft and the cabin starts at the checkpoint floor (src/types.ts:505-536).

g87 · scoreboard. The five outcomes as a match score, each with its one-line cause (src/types.ts:465-499).

g88 · gears. The pump as meshing gears: waits engage, resume engages, plain commits spin free. Borrowed invariant: engagement, not motion (src/harness/scheduler.ts:1096-1112).

g89 · punch tape. The jsonl adapter as a sprocketed tape: entries in order, one writer, one needle. Borrowed invariant: append-only (src/types.ts:416-443).

12 · HONESTWhat this catalog did and did not verify

honest status

Every fact drawn here was read and receipted in the session page (docs/pidurable) or is a lab result from this repo (swarm-lab). The catalog re-uses those facts to isolate the grammars — it adds no new claims. All 101 figures passed the geometric checker (only a real browser can say where text lands); the screenshots were additionally read section by section. Grammars shown as static shapes (treemap, sunburst, flame, gantt, b-tree) are hand-placed schematics, not rendered by layout algorithms — their honesty is the caption's claim, not a tool's output.
